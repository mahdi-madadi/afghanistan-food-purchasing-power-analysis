## LinkedIn or portfolio version

I analyzed World Food Programme market data to examine how much low-quality wheat flour one reported paid day of work could purchase across 34 monitored Afghan markets. The project covers May 2020 to December 2025 and combines Excel data-quality review, Power BI dashboard development, and Python validation. The median purchasing-power indicator fell to 5.78 kg per paid workday in June 2022, reached 12.57 kg in July 2024, and ended at 10.04 kg in December 2025. I documented limitations carefully: the indicator measures the food value of a paid workday, not employment availability or household welfare.

## Interview summary

"I built a reproducible market-monitoring project using WFP data from Afghanistan. I joined daily wages and flour prices by market and month, calculated kilograms of flour purchasable with one paid workday, summarized the ratios with monthly medians, reviewed unusual movements, and validated the Power BI results in Python. The main lesson was that the indicator is useful for monitoring affordability, but it cannot tell us whether people found work or whether a household had enough food overall."
